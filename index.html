About Jimma

Jimma is a historic city in southwestern Ethiopia, known for its rich Oromo culture, coffee production, trade, and Islamic heritage. Its history is closely connected to the Five Gibe Kingdoms, which emerged in the region during the late 18th and early 19th centuries. The Kingdom of Jimma became prominent under Abba Jifar I and reached its peak under Abba Jifar II. During the 19th century, Jimma developed into an important center of commerce and Islamic learning. In 1932, the Kingdom of Jimma lost its autonomy and was incorporated into the Ethiopian Empire. Today, Jimma is an important economic, educational, and cultural center, widely recognized for its contribution to Ethiopia’s coffee industry.

The Five Kingdom of Jimma

  1. Kingdom of Jimma Jimma became one of the most influential kingdoms in the region under Abba Jifar I and Abba Jifar II. Its economy benefited from agriculture, coffee and regional commerce. It also developed a strong royal administration and became an important center of Islamic learning.
  2. Kingdom of Limmu-Ennarea Limmu-Ennarea was among the earliest and initially strongest of the Gibe kingdoms. Its strategic location helped it control important long-distance trade routes. Its influential ruler, Abba Bagibo, governed during much of the first half of the 19th century.
  3. Kingdom of Gomma Gomma was one of the five Oromo monarchies that emerged as local leadership became more centralized. Its political development was connected to the wider changes in agriculture, trade, settlement and Islam across the Gibe region.
  4. Kingdom of Gera Gera was another independent Oromo kingdom in the Gibe region. Like its neighbors, it developed its own political leadership and participated in the regional economy. The surrounding highlands and agricultural lands formed part of the environment in which these communities lived and traded.
  5. Kingdom of Gumma (Guma) Gumma was located west of the other Gibe states, near the Didessa River region. It emerged as a kingdom through competition and political consolidation among local Oromo communities.

Jimma University Welcomes Freshman Students

Jimma University welcomed its newly admitted freshman students on Wednesday and Thursday, October 14–15, 2026, as part of the registration process for the 2026/2027 academic year. According to the registration announcement, Natural Sciences students were assigned to register at the Jimma Institute of Technology, while Social Sciences students were directed to the College of Agriculture and Veterinary Medicine. The university community welcomes the new students and wishes them success as they begin their academic journey.
